About

I am a PhD candidate at the Indian Institute of Technology Mandi, where I work with Dr. Vivek Gupta in the HIMPACT Lab. My research uses generative deep learning, mainly diffusion and flow matching, to downscale climate and remote sensing data over India. I care about what these models get right beyond pointwise error: uncertainty, extremes, fine-scale structure and transfer to regions outside the training domain.

From October 2026 to January 2027 I am a DAAD GROW visiting researcher with Prof. Niklas Boers at the Technical University of Munich, working on a diffusion emulator of kilometre-scale regional climate simulations over the Alps.

I am looking for postdoctoral positions starting in mid-2027. I am most interested in generative models for regional climate emulation and downscaling, uncertainty quantification and extremes, transfer across mountain regions, and machine learning for weather forecasting and forecast post-processing.

Before my PhD I studied statistics (BSc, Central University of Rajasthan) and geoinformatics (MSc, TERI School of Advanced Studies).

Research

Probabilistic temperature downscaling with diffusion

Manuscript under review. Dataset released.

A deterministic network trained on squared error predicts the conditional mean, so it smooths away the variance and tails of local temperature. I couple a conditional diffusion model (DDPM) with quantile delta mapping to downscale daily near-surface temperature from 1° to 0.1° over India, for ten CMIP6 models and three emission scenarios out to 2100.

Dataset on Zenodo

  1. Every day is an ensemble

    Instead of a single answer, the diffusion model samples many plausible 0.1° fields for each day. Their spread maps where the downscaled value is uncertain, and like the error it is largest over complex terrain.

  2. Keeping the fine scales

    A deterministic residual U-Net trained under the same conditions has a lower single-sample error, but it loses spatial detail: over India it falls below half of the observed power at intermediate scales. The diffusion model keeps the observed spectrum down to the 0.1° grid.

  3. From climate model to local map

    Raw climate model output carries biases of several degrees. Each model is first corrected with quantile delta mapping and then downscaled, which brings the remaining bias down to a fraction of a degree.

  4. Honest about uncertainty

    The ensemble is informative but underdispersed: the U-shaped rank histograms show observations falling outside the ensemble range more often than they should. I report this openly, because it decides how the dataset should be used.

Diffusion and flow matching for precipitation extremes

Manuscript under review. Dataset released.

Precipitation is intermittent and heavy-tailed, and most of its impact sits in the upper tail. I benchmark diffusion and flow matching against three deterministic methods for 10× daily precipitation downscaling over India, with a 14-year test period.

Dataset on Zenodo

  1. A fair test with a known answer

    I coarsen observed precipitation from 0.1° to 1.0° and ask each method to recover the original. Diffusion and flow matching share one U-Net; constructed analogues, weather typing and SRCNN are the deterministic baselines.

  2. The average looks right for everyone

    Averaged over 14 years, every method reproduces the broad pattern of the Western Ghats, the northeast and the Himalayan foothills. The differences hide in the daily fields.

  3. Where the detail goes

    The spectrum shows how much spatial detail survives at each scale. The deterministic and analogue methods lose power well before the grid scale. Flow matching stays closest to the observed spectrum, and diffusion is somewhat smoother.

  4. Only the generative models get the extremes

    Diffusion and flow matching follow the observed return levels of the heaviest daily rain. The other methods compress the upper tail, which is exactly the part that matters for flood risk.

  5. The monsoon's rhythm

    Beyond totals, I test whether each method keeps the alternation between active and break phases of the summer monsoon, using composites of the observed phase days.

  6. Fidelity at lower cost

    Flow matching reaches comparable fidelity with far fewer network evaluations than diffusion, so large ensembles become cheaper to produce.

CNN super-resolution for temperature

Published in Frontiers in Climate, 2025.

My first PhD study compared SRCNN, VDSR and EDSR for downscaling ERA5 2 m temperature by a factor of 10 over the Indian subcontinent, with residual networks for the steep terrain of the Himalaya and the Tibetan Plateau.

Ongoing work

  • Emulating kilometre-scale climate simulations over the Alps

    In progress at TU Munich

    A latent diffusion model that emulates 3 km WRF regional climate simulations (CHAPTER) over the Alps, predicting 2 m temperature and 10 m wind from ERA5, terrain and land cover.

    Next: testing transfer outside the training region, as a step towards the Himalaya.

  • Where spatial resolution matters for cooling demand

    Manuscript in preparation

    Coarse grids misestimate impact indices that respond nonlinearly to temperature. I show where and by how much, using local curvature and subgrid variance, for cooling degree days over India.

  • Density-based diagnostics for generative downscalers

    Manuscript in preparation

    Diagnostics for a flow matching precipitation downscaler, validated on synthetic data with known ground truth.
